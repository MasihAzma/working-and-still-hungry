Toronto food banks · April 2024 to March 2025

Hunger in Toronto has a job, a student card and a rent bill.

Toronto food banks recorded 4.1 million visits last year, the most ever. Almost half of the people behind those visits live in a household where someone works. Hunger here is a housing and income problem, and City Council can act on it in the 2027 budget.

Who is hungry

The person in line might be your co-worker or classmate

Most people still picture food bank users as people without jobs. The survey of Toronto food bank clients shows something different.

The need is getting deeper, not just wider

For the first time since 2020, most visits (59%) came from returning clients. More than half of clients (54%) now visit three or more times a month, up from 43% the year before. People are not using food banks for a short emergency anymore. They depend on them.

The root causes are income and housing costs, not a lack of food. That is why our ask goes to City Council: support the Toronto Poverty Reduction Strategy and affordable housing in the 2027 budget.

Data brief

Working and Still Hungry: key numbers
  • 4,124,313 food bank visits in Toronto, April 2024 to March 2025: up 18% in a year and 340% since before the pandemic.
  • More than 1 in 10 Torontonians rely on a food bank; 112,554 used one for the first time.
  • 46% of clients live in a household where someone works; 23% are students; 1 in 4 are children.
  • 71% of clients spend more than half their income on housing.
  • 59% of visits came from returning clients; 54% of clients visit three or more times a month (up from 43%).
  • Canada-wide, 24.0% of people (about 9.8 million) lived in food-insecure households in 2025.
